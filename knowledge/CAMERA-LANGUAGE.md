# Camera language

Use this as a vocabulary library, not as a menu to stack everything together.

## Shot size

- extreme macro / detail
- extreme close-up
- close-up
- medium close-up
- medium
- medium wide
- wide / establishing
- overhead / top-down
- low-angle hero
- high-angle observational

## Support and behavior

- locked tripod — no drift, architectural/product precision
- slider — short controlled lateral move
- dolly — physical push/pull with perspective change
- gimbal — stable following movement
- handheld — human micro-motion; specify restrained vs nervous
- crane/jib — vertical spatial reveal
- vehicle/drone follow — large-scale tracking

## Primary movements

- slow push-in
- pull-back reveal
- lateral track
- arc/orbit
- pan
- tilt
- rise / descend
- lead/follow tracking
- rack focus without moving the camera

## Reliable combinations

- locked frame + rack focus
- slow push-in + very small pan correction
- 15-30 degree product orbit + subtle dolly-in
- lateral track + subject walking parallel
- crane-down + settle into a locked composition

## Risky combinations

- full 360 orbit around a complex human interaction
- whip pan into a macro product shot while the product transforms
- simultaneous zoom + dolly + orbit + roll
- fast handheld camera plus fine hand interaction plus exact product geometry

## Lens language

Use lens feel only when it affects composition:

- 24mm: environmental, perspective expansion
- 35mm: natural wide cinematic coverage
- 50mm: neutral human perspective
- 85mm: compressed portrait/product detail
- macro: fine material/condensation/texture

Avoid specifying exact cinema camera brands unless their appearance is actually relevant. Describe the image behavior you need.

## Camera endpoint

Every move should end somewhere:

> Slow 25-degree clockwise orbit that settles into a centered three-quarter product view.

That is substantially more controllable than:

> cinematic orbiting camera.
